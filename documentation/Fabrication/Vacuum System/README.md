# Vacuum System Assembly

**Parts required:**

- See vacuum parts list section in main parts spreadsheet.

### Unpacking Components

![s0](<./Images/Vacuum_Chamber/s0.png>)

If already installed, remove the RGA electronics enclosure from the Quadropole tube by unscrewing the long body screws and sliding/pulling the case back firmly.

![s1](<./Images/Vacuum_Chamber/s1.jpg>)

If the turbopump has a small DIN flange attached to the outlet port, unscrew it using a wrench on the flat sides.

### Assembling the Vacuum Stackup

While assembling the vacuum components, make sure your work area is relatively dust/residue free, wear fresh gloves, and avoid touching inner surfaces that will be exposed to vacuum where possible.

> [!tip]
>
> Have a sturdy chemistry lab stand with a flask clamp, affixed to the table you're working on to provide a second grip to keep the vacuum stack upright as it grows.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> ALWAYS USE FRESH COPPER GASKETS whenever you must open, re-tighten or re-assemble any vacuum flanges!!

![s2](<./Images/Vacuum_Chamber/s2.png>)

With the turbo pump upright, lay a fresh copper gasket on the circular "kife edge" followed by the 2.75" to 4.5" Conflat flange adapter and align the holes.

![s3](<./Images/Vacuum_Chamber/s3.png>)

Screw together with the silver plated 5/16-24 x 2.00" bolts untill just snug, but don't tighten yet! Use Nordlock/wedgelock washers on the back side between nuts and bolt holes. Do not use washers on the bolt head side since the bolt head flare already distributes the load.

> [!NOTE]
>
> The silver plating on the bolts serves two important functions: It lubricates the bolts and provides a dissimilar metal to prevent galling without the need for lubricants which may off-gas and contaminate the mass spec signal.

![s4](<./Images/Vacuum_Chamber/s4.png>)

Stack the rest of the vacuum chamber components into a small tower, each with a fresh 2.75" CFF copper gasket. Pay attention to the alignment of several key features: The turbo pump control box and side port on the second small flange facing the front side, while the two long alignment pins on the RGA quad tube are towards the back in this illustration. Lower the quad tube gently to avoid hitting the cage on the inside of the vacuum flanges.

![s5](<./Images/Vacuum_Chamber/s5.png>)


Screw together till finger-snug using the silver plated 1/4-28 X 2-1/2" bolts that were order along with a Stainless steel normal washer.

### Tightening Bolts


![s6B](<./Images/Vacuum_Chamber/s5B.png>)

Once you are satisfied that everything is properly rotated and aligned, use the fixed lab stand mentioned earlier to hold the Quadropole tube and keep everything steady

![s6](<./Images/Vacuum_Chamber/s6.png>)

while tightening all the bolts we've installed so far (so both the bolts in the 4.5" CF flange and 2.75" CF flanges).

When tightening conflat flanges, tighten every other bolt in an alternating pattern. Use a long wrench or wench extension to get maximum leverage. When done right you should be able to keep tightening each set of bolts past where they used to feel stuck many times - this is the copper gaskets deforming under the stress - which is what we want to get a perfect seal. A fully tightened conflat flange pair should have a gap only slightly larger than a sheet of paper- You'll know you are done when you physically can't tighten ANY of the bolts with a long wrench.

> [!TIP]
>
> To gain leverage on the turbo pump without scratching it, lay a second small wrench between the star bolt heads on the 4.5" base so the wrench handle/bar gets stuck between the boltheads and the conflat flange outer surfaces and allows you to put torque on it.

### Adding Tube Fittings

![s7](<./Images/Vacuum_Chamber/s7.png>)

Spread a small amount of swagelok VAC Goop on the BSPP threads of the BSPP to 1/4" Yorlock tubing fitting to prevent galling.

Twist BSPP threads into the port until close to the right angle, then finger tighten the loose nut down onto the fitting to seal temporally.

> [!IMPORTANT]
>
> BSPP, yorlock tube, and swagelok tube are not tappered thread fittings, so DON'T USE PTFE TAPE / "TEFLON" TAPE on these threads - it won't help with sealing and could hurt it.

![s8](<./Images/Vacuum_Chamber/s8.png>)



Note for future steps: The bspp fitting is angled slightly down to accommodate the tubing run. Get it roughly aligned now, with the idea that you'll need to loosen the lower nut and get the angle exact when you install the tubing run shown in orange later.

![s9](<./Images/Vacuum_Chamber/s9.png>)



Screw the in the 1/8" NPT to 1/8" swagelock tube fitting into the side port. Wrap with 2-4 wraps of teflon tape. You may need to play with the number of wraps to get the angle right.

> [!IMPORTANT]
>
> When working with NPT fittings, always use 2 or more wraps of standard thickness Teflon/PTFE tape (or slightly less with 'stainless steel' rated PTFE tape). The biggest risk is getting bits of teflon tape inside the vacuum chamber which can fly into the turbo pump and jam or dent the blades. SO, make sure the teflon tape wrap doesn't extend past the last screw thread, and that you clean the inner and outer threads after removing teflon-taped fitting with a pick before re-inserting a new fitting with new teflon tape. Orient the chamber down so any tape bits don't fall into the vacuum chamber while cleaning it.

### Bending Vacuum Tubing Run

Parts & tools required:

- 1/4" OD x  Stainless steel continuous straight tubing - about 4ft long.
- 1/4" tubing bend tool
- Protractor, Pen & Paper
- Clamps and a flat work surface.

These are the steps to create the tubing run that goes between the Roughing inlet and Turbo Pump outlet shown here: https://cad.onshape.com/documents/b6c7e603ca3691b93cbc85d9/w/9ed404cfa2f2ee86ebc2e394/e/7f697bb443b9acb569f2a20e

1. Starting from one end of the tube, mark off the following distances (all relative to your 'start' end of the tube)

   | **Distance from start** | **Bend amount AFTER each mark**                              |
   | ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
   | 82mm                    | 15° - PLANE A                                                |
   | 139mm                   | -15° - PLANE A                                               |
   | 186mm                   | 8.43° - PLANE B (PLANE B is perpendicular to PLANE A, Rotated 90° along the axis of the tube) |
   | 332mm                   | 45° - PLANE B                                                |
   | 395mm                   | -90° - PLANE B                                               |
   | 460mm                   | 90° - PLANE B                                                |
   | 526mm                   | -90° - PLANE B                                               |
   | 600mm                   | 45° - PLANE B                                                |
   | 659mm                   | 90° - PLANE C - (perpendicular to both plane A & B; axially rotated 59° CCW relative to PLANE B (or 31° CCW relative to PLANE A)) |
   | 764mm                   | -90° - PLANE C                                               |
   | 807mm                   | END CUT                                                      |
